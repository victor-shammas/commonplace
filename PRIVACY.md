# Commonplace privacy policy

_Last updated: October 11, 2026_

Commonplace is a Chrome extension that saves the page you are viewing to a note in Apple Notes on your Mac. It does not collect, transmit or sell any personal data.

## What Commonplace reads

- **The page you save.** When you open Commonplace, it reads the active tab's title and address (URL). It also reads any text you have selected on the page, to fill in the quick note.
- **What you type.** The quick note you write.
- **Your note and folder names.** To let you pick a note, it lists the names, folders and modification dates of your Apple Notes. When saving, the helper checks the text of the note open in Notes to confirm it is the one you picked. It doesn't store or send the contents of your notes.

## Where that data goes

All of this stays on your Mac. When you click Save, the extension passes the page title, URL, timestamp and your quick note to the Commonplace helper installed on your Mac. The helper adds them to the note you chose. To do that, it briefly places the entry on your clipboard and pastes it into Apple Notes, then restores your clipboard's previous contents.

The list of note and folder names, your last-used folder and note, and your settings are stored only in Chrome's local storage for the extension, on your device.

Commonplace makes no network requests. It has no analytics, tracking or advertising, and nothing is sent to the developer or any third party. Apple Notes may sync your notes through iCloud according to your own Apple settings. Commonplace has no part in that.

## Removing your data

Uninstalling the extension deletes its local storage. Uninstalling the helper removes everything else Commonplace installed. The entries you saved stay in your notes until you delete them.
